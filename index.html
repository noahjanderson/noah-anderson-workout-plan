The Week

Tap a day to see the exercises. Today's day is highlighted and opened for you.

MondayChest / Push Today
  • Cable flies, high to low squeeze at the center
  • Cable flies, low to high upper chest
  • Incline press dumbbells or barbell
  • Flat bench press dumbbells or barbell
  • Finish: fly machine, push-ups, or dips

Cardio: 10–15 min stair climber

TuesdayBack / Pull Today
  • Pull-ups to warm up optional
  • Chest-supported seated row chest on the pad, pull, squeeze, hold, slow return
  • Lat pulldowns strict, no rocking, pull to chest, slow return
  • Bent-over dumbbell rows (flat bench) or straight-bar cable pulldowns to the knees

Tip: wrist straps can help you focus on the lats.

Cardio: 10–15 min

WednesdayShoulders Today
  • Lateral raises lead with the elbows, pinkies up, squeeze the delts
  • Bent-over rear delt flies
  • Cable face pulls rope to the face
  • Single-arm cable side raises lean away from the stack
  • Military press

Cardio: 10–15 min

ThursdayLegs Today
  • Hamstring curls try 21s
  • Leg extensions 21s work here too
  • Hip thrusts clean motion, squeeze at the top, don't overextend the back
  • Barbell squats or leg press / sled machine if you have lower back concerns
  • Calf raises

Cardio: 10–15 min

FridayArms Today
  • Back-against-the-wall curls
  • Incline dumbbell curls
  • Seated hammer curls
  • Cable triceps pushdowns with the V-bar strict, shoulders forward, try 21s
  • Cable overhead / arm-rest extensions for a triceps stretch
  • Forearms: cable wrist curls, then reverse-grip forearm rolls

Cardio: 10–15 min

SaturdayCore Today
  • Cable crunches heavy, slight side-to-side, ankles together, slow and controlled
  • Reverse crunches / ab machine
  • Incline sit-ups 10–15 reps, add weight over time
  • Planks 60 seconds or more

Lower abs

  • Hanging leg raises to L-sit hold the last rep
  • Hanging knee raises hold the last rep
  • Decline leg raises

Obliques

  • Russian twists
  • Cable twists at chest height
  • Side planks

Tip: swap the upper / lower ab order from week to week.

Cardio: 10–15 min

SundayRest Today

Recover. Sleep, eat well, go for a walk, stretch if you feel like it. Rest is part of the plan.

The “21s” explained

  1. 7 reps in the bottom half
  2. 7 reps in the top half
  3. 7 full reps

No rest between them. A great finisher for curls, triceps, hamstrings, and quads.

Training notes

  • 3–4 sets per exercise.
  • Around 7 reps if lifting heavy, 10–12 reps with lighter weight.
  • Control the weight, squeeze at the contraction, no momentum.

New to lifting?

Start Here

  • Pick 3–4 exercises per day, not the whole list.
  • 2–3 sets of 10–12 reps with light weight. Form beats weight.
  • Start with 2–3 days a week, not 6.
  • Machines and cables are beginner friendly because they guide the movement.
  • Skip 21s and heavy sets for the first few weeks.
  • Ask someone who lifts if you're unsure about a movement.

Sources

These back up the general guidance (how often to train, set and rep ranges, starting light). They don't prove my specific exercise choices.

  1. CDC, Adult Activity: An Overview. Adults need 150 minutes a week of moderate aerobic activity plus muscle-strengthening activity on 2 or more days a week that works all major muscle groups.
  2. Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health, New Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans. A summary of the HHS 2nd edition guidelines.
  3. American College of Sports Medicine, Progression Models in Resistance Training for Healthy Adults (position stand, 2009). 2–3 days a week for novices, 8–12 rep ranges for novice training, and gradual progression.
  4. ACSM, Resistance Training Prescription position stand update (2026, PDF), which updates the 2009 stand. About 2–3 sets per exercise for strength and roughly 10+ sets per muscle per week for muscle growth.
  5. Garber et al., ACSM Position Stand, Quantity and Quality of Exercise for Developing and Maintaining Fitness in Adults (2011, summary PDF). Single-set training for novices and 2–4 sets for strength.

Tips like 21s, face pull cues, and exercise order are my personal experience, not research-backed claims.
